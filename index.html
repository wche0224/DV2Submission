FIG. 08 / 11GEOGRAPHY / CLUSTERS

Where Does Comic Publishing Cluster?

Reveal regional concentrations by combining nearby headquarters into equal-area hexagons.

Calculating key insight…

How the hexagons are calculated ↗

How to read this: Each hexagon groups nearby headquarters locations using an equal-area projection. Darker colours indicate higher counts of recorded publisher-headquarters associations. The underlying grid has an approximately 500 km centre-to-corner hexagon radius in the projected coordinate system. Sources: Wikidata and Natural Earth. Only locations with available coordinates are included. Missing records do not indicate an absence of comic publishers.

CHAPTER03

Behind the spotlight

Fame is far from equal

Some characters return again and again while many appear only rarely. Distributions uncover what top-ten rankings cannot.

FIG. 09 / 11DISTRIBUTION / APPEARANCES

Are Heroes More Frequently Seen?

Look beyond total counts to compare typical appearances and variation across alignments.

Calculating key insight…

Reading the box plots ↗

How to read this: The line inside each box represents the median number of appearances. The box contains the middle 50% of character appearance counts. Whiskers extend to values within 1.5 times the interquartile range. Individual outlier points are hidden for visual clarity. Logarithmic scale: Equal horizontal distances represent tenfold increases in appearances. Source: FiveThirtyEight Comic Characters, historical 2014 dataset. Only known positive appearance counts for Good, Bad and Neutral characters are included.

FIG. 10 / 11DENSITY / APPEARANCES

Are Most Characters Rarely Seen?

Compare the long-tailed shapes of appearance distributions for Marvel and DC.

Calculating key insight…

Understanding the density curves ↗

How to read this: Each ridge represents one character group. Taller parts indicate a greater relative concentration of characters at that appearance count. The horizontal axis uses a logarithmic scale. Curves are smoothed estimates, not exact frequency counts. Each group has a normalised density, so ridge size does not represent the total number of characters. Source: FiveThirtyEight Comic Characters, historical 2014 dataset. Only Good, Bad and Neutral characters with known positive appearances are included.

FIG. 11 / 11INEQUALITY / APPEARANCES

Do a Few Characters Dominate?

See how much of each publisher’s recorded appearances belongs to its most frequent characters.

Calculating key insight…

How to read the concentration curves ↗

How to read this: Characters are ranked from most to least recorded appearances. The horizontal axis shows the top percentage of characters included. The vertical axis shows their combined share of recorded appearances. A steeper initial rise indicates greater concentration among leading characters. The dashed grey diagonal represents a hypothetical equal distribution. The vertical reference highlights the top 10% of characters. Source: FiveThirtyEight Comic Characters, historical 2014 dataset. Only characters with known positive appearance counts are included. Percentage groups are rounded up to whole character counts.

THE SOURCE FILES

Behind the data

Comic-character data from FiveThirtyEight Comic Characters (historical data through 2014). Publisher and headquarters information from Wikidata, cross-referenced through Grand Comics Database identifiers. Country boundaries from Natural Earth via vega-datasets.

Method: Python scripts prepare aggregated JSON and geographic data; Vega-Lite renders every figure. Colour shows categories or ordered magnitudes as appropriate. Missing publisher records should not be interpreted as zero publishers.
